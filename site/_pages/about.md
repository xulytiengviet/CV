I am **Long Ngo** (Ngô Hoàng Đại Long / Dai-Long Ngo-Hoang), a researcher working at the intersection of **environmental geography, geographic information science, artificial intelligence, sensing, and autonomous systems**. My current work is associated with **VNUHCM – Vinh Long Campus** and is strongly oriented toward open, reproducible geospatial research and engineering.

My training connects several fields that are often treated separately: a **B.Sc. in Environmental Geography (2008)**, an **M.Sc. in GIS (2014)**, a **Ph.D. in IoT (2023)**, and postdoctoral research in 2026. This path shaped a research agenda in which places, sensors, language models, maps, and embodied machines are treated as parts of one computational geographic system.

## Research focus

My long-term research program connects five closely related directions:

- **Environmental Geography** — spatial processes, natural resources, climate and environmental applications.
- **GIS & GeoAI** — spatial data infrastructure, WebGIS, geocomputation, spatial reasoning and AI-assisted GIS.
- **Drone & Robotics** — field mapping, navigation, autonomous sensing and embodied geospatial intelligence.
- **NLP & LLM** — Vietnamese language technology, geospatial question answering, tool-using agents and knowledge interfaces.
- **3S: State Estimation · Sensor Fusion · SLAM** — the bridge from observations and coordinates to reliable machine action in real environments.

A parallel technical thread is **sensing and identification**, evolving from RFID toward chipless RFID, compact sensing systems and edge intelligence.

## Research approach

I am especially interested in systems that move beyond “GIS calling AI” toward **AI orchestrating geospatial tools**: an intelligent layer that can understand a spatial question, plan the required operations, call GIS/data services, validate results, and communicate the outcome through maps, explanations or robot actions.

This portfolio is designed as a living research interface. Publications, software, datasets, interactive maps and experiments will be added progressively, with GitHub used as the reproducible backbone.
